\section*{DOOM Quality Modernization: Before vs. After}

This report uses an additive policy. The general host/native profile is not
replaced by the guest-C profile, and future \texttt{malbolge-*} checks will add
to the result rather than erase existing diagnostics.

The generated corpus is clean for the current guest-C bootstrap, all 390
multi-TU strict compiler checks, and all six single-TU checks. It is not clean
under the complete general profile: 36,519 unique findings remain.

Before corpus: \texttt{doom}.\\
After corpus:
\texttt{algorithms/doom/quality/out/doom\_fixed}.\\
Pinned validator toolchain: LLVM/Clang 22.1.8.

\subsection*{Why 36,519 findings remain}
\begin{tabular}{lrl}
\toprule
Class & Count & Interpretation \\
\midrule
Literal and table constants & 22303 & strict policy debt \\
Historical naming and length & 3314 & source/linkage migration \\
Braces, includes, format, text & 4667 & mechanical debt \\
Signed bitwise operations & 1461 & priority portability review \\
Initialization and globals & 2698 & architectural debt \\
Remaining rules & 2076 & mixed review debt \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The 22,303 magic-number reports are dominated by renderer tables, fixed-point
constants, protocol values, and historical game data. They are real violations
of the configured rule, but not independent runtime defects.

Naming and identifier-length findings cover historical APIs and pervasive
short mathematical names. Bulk renaming would be a source and linkage migration,
not a compatibility repair.

Braces, direct includes, formatting, and text layout are actionable mechanical
debt. They remain counted because no blanket suppression is accepted.

Signed bitwise findings deserve correctness review. Many sites are deliberate
fixed-point or bit-level operations, but each needs an unsigned-domain proof or
rewrite. Initialization, mutable-global, complexity, and function-size rules
require structural modernization beyond this compatibility transform.

\subsection*{Priority findings resolved in this regeneration}
The focused analyzer/bugprone pass now reports zero array-bound findings, dead
stores, implementation-defined narrowing conversions, signed-char misuse,
assignments or increments hidden in conditions, unparenthesized replacement
macros, and late widening of multiplication results.

One concrete array-bound defect was fixed in the intermission map renderer. A
single splat pointer was passed to code that always attempted two candidates.
The new API carries the candidate count explicitly and cannot inspect a missing
second pointer.

The three remaining interoperability heuristic findings do not mean host
support is absent. Two expect platform-specific Windows/macOS source inside the
guest tree even though the design uses one host-neutral capability ABI. The
third is a stale \texttt{sndserver} comment.
